# AI & tooling in practice

The `newTools` (daily) and `toolsToWatch` (weekly) section: real uses of AI and other tooling that a reader can learn from or reproduce. Not a product-launch feed.

## Belongs

- Videos where the creator actually builds or runs something.
- Repositories and examples someone can clone and run, including small or odd projects.
- Show HN and similar posts with a working demo or source.
- Writeups and case studies of a real workflow, with enough detail to follow; documented experiments, even ones that partly fail.
- Integrations: models, agents, editors, CLIs, hardware, data pipelines or automation wired together.

Cover AI and other worthwhile tooling: software engineering, automation, 3D, video, data, local models, design and more.

## Doesn't belong

Launches, pricing and funding (news sections, if significant); sponsored or affiliate content and "top 10" lists; demos with no code, steps or method; README-only repos; anything already in another section of the edition. A launch qualifies only when someone else built something notable with it and documented how.

## Finding them

Every day, try four paths: **YouTube** (check real upload dates; smaller creators too), **Show HN**, **GitHub** (trending, topic pages, recently updated examples) and **working demos and experiments** ([Hugging Face Spaces](https://huggingface.co/spaces?sort=trending), [Simon Willison's coding-agent notes](https://simonwillison.net/tags/coding-agents/), independent blogs). On Sundays, also use `agent/catalog/sunday-maker-venues.md`. Rotate themes across the week (coding agents, 3D, game engines, simulation, creative coding, local inference, data viz, automation, hardware, audio and video) and search outside known venues.

If a path is unavailable, record it in the audit and use another.

## Choosing

Judge hands-on evidence, reproducibility, originality, practical value, independence from the vendor, and freshness within the window. No quota and no cap: include everything that clears the bar, and nothing that doesn't.

## Verifying

- Watch or read the material itself, not the title, thumbnail or README. For long videos, say which parts you reviewed. A video you couldn't watch can't support a detailed briefing.
- For repos: the code exists and matches the claim; license, recent commits, setup instructions, open failure reports.
- Note what reproducing it requires: paid APIs, hardware, accounts, model versions.
- Never claim you ran something; say "not independently reproduced".
- Link the video, repo, demo or writeup directly, with labels like `YouTube tutorial`, `Source code`, `Live demo`, `Writeup`.

## Writing

- `summary` (60–120 words): what was built or shown, the key technique and the evidence.
- `format` (optional, max 40 characters): `Video tutorial`, `Workflow`, `Experiment`, `Case study`, `Demo`, `Show HN`…
- `briefing` (2–6 sections, 120–300 words): pick from What was done, How it works, Why it's interesting, How to reproduce it, Limitations. Don't invent steps.
- `limitations`: caveats, unverified claims, what you couldn't access.
